JumpToTech Bank — four services, one of them invisible
======================================================

Four things on this host hold sockets. Nobody on the platform team can say
which is which, and one of them — payments-api — is being reported as "down"
by everything on the segment while the engineer who deployed it insists it is
running. Both are true, which is the whole problem.

Take the inventory first. For every socket on this host you want four facts:

  · the protocol
  · the port
  · the address it is bound to
  · whether it is a listener or an established connection

The last two are where the answer is. A port number on its own tells you
almost nothing: two sockets can hold the same port and be entirely different
things, and a listener is identified by more than the number it answers on.


WHAT "BOUND TO" DECIDES
-----------------------

A listening socket is bound to an address, and that address decides who can
ever reach it. One of the addresses you will find here can only be reached by
the host itself, no matter how healthy the service behind it is, how open the
network is, or how many times it is restarted.

That is not a bug in the service. It is the service doing exactly what it was
configured to do.


THE REPAIR
----------

payments-api reads its own configuration at startup:

  /etc/payments/api.conf

Change it so the service is reachable from the segment this host is on, not
only from the host itself, and restart it so the change takes effect. `sv` is
the supervisor here; `sv restart <service>` and `sv status <service>` are the
two forms you are likely to want. The services live in /etc/service.

Leave the other three alone. A repair that takes down a healthy service is not
a repair, and the inventory you took at the start is how you will know.


A NOTE ON WHAT YOU WILL SEE
---------------------------

Two of the sockets on this host belong to the container runtime rather than to
the bank, and one of the rows is a connection rather than a listener. Working
out which rows are yours to care about is part of taking an inventory.
