JumpToTech Bank — "just retry on failure"
=========================================

A team was told to retry on failure. They now retry a service that is refusing
connections, several thousand times a minute, and the incident channel has
decided the network is flaky.

Nothing is flaky. One of the three things they are talking to answers, one
takes messages and keeps no state at all, and one refuses instantly — and the
difference is visible on the wire if you look.

Your job is to look, and then to be able to say what a connection actually is.


WHAT IS ON THIS HOST
--------------------

  ledger-echo      TCP, port 9200 — accepts connections and echoes
  ledger-metrics   UDP, port 9201 — takes datagrams
  port 9202        nothing is listening here

Leave all three as they are. This lab is about observing them, not repairing
them.


A NOTE ON WHERE THE TRAFFIC GOES
--------------------------------

You will be talking to services on this host, so address them on this host.
Traffic to an address that belongs to the machine sending it never reaches the
network interface — the kernel delivers it internally, over the loopback
interface. Capture on the wrong interface and you will see a great deal of
nothing.

You have the privilege to capture on this sandbox. That is deliberate and it
is narrow: this session has its own private segment with nothing else on it,
so the only traffic you can see is your own.


THE THREE EXCHANGES
-------------------

  handshake    Open a TCP connection to 9200, send something, let it close.
               Capture it. You are looking for the three packets that open a
               connection before any data moves, and the flags that name them.

  refused      Try to connect to 9202. Capture it. Compare how long it takes
               and what comes back with the handshake above.

  udp          Send a datagram to 9201. Capture it. Count the packets that
               precede your data.

Then look at the socket table while and after the TCP connection is open —
a connection outlives the close, and the state it sits in afterwards has a
name and a reason.


WHAT TO RECORD
--------------

  handshake.txt    the capture of the exchange with 9200
  refused.txt      the capture of the attempt against 9202
  udp.txt          the capture of the datagram to 9201
  states.txt       the socket table showing the connection after it closed

Then complete answers.txt, which is already here.
