JumpToTech Bank — payment gateway, link-layer review
====================================================

The team's mental model of the payment gateway stops at "it has an IP". That
held until last week, when a host with a perfectly correct address could not
be reached and nobody could say why, because nobody could say what happens
between "send this to that address" and a frame leaving the interface.

This host is on a small private segment of its own. Nothing else is on it
except the segment's gateway, and there is no route off it — which is
convenient, because it means you can produce all three outcomes below without
touching anything real.

Your job is to make the host resolve a neighbour, fail to resolve one, and
then ask for something it cannot even try to resolve. The kernel records all
three, and what it records is the evidence.


FIRST, FIND YOUR OWN SEGMENT
----------------------------

The addressing here is allocated when your lab starts, so it is not written
down anywhere — you have to read it off the machine. You need three things:

  · the name of your non-loopback interface
  · your own address and prefix length
  · the address of the gateway on your segment

`ip addr` and `ip route` between them will tell you all three.


A NOTE ON TOOLS
---------------

`ping` will not work here. It needs a privilege this sandbox does not grant,
and that is deliberate: the point is what the kernel does when *anything*
tries to send, not what one tool does.

Use a TCP connection attempt instead. It does not matter that nothing is
listening — the kernel has to resolve the neighbour before it can send the
first packet, which is the part you are here to watch. Any of these work:

    nc -w2 -v <address> 9
    socat -T2 - TCP:<address>:9

The connection will fail. That is fine, and expected, in all three cases.


THE THREE OUTCOMES
------------------

  resolved     Reach for the gateway. It is on your segment and it answers,
               so the kernel learns its hardware address.

  unanswered   Reach for some other address inside your own prefix — one you
               can see from your addressing that nothing is using. The kernel
               asks, nothing answers, and it records that.

  no route     Reach for 10.99.99.99, which is outside your prefix and has no
               route. Notice what the kernel does *not* do here.


WHAT TO RECORD
--------------

`ip neigh` shows the table the kernel keeps. Save it, once you have produced
all three outcomes, to:

  findings.txt

Then complete `answers.txt`, which is already in this directory.
